# Process

Throughout the writing process, I used ChatGPT and Doubao to help me brainstorm ideas, structure the essay, find relevant sources, and revise the language and organisation of the final text. I used VS Code to write and edit the Markdown files, and Git and GitHub to track different stages of the essay and run the assignment check.

At the beginning, my argument was still quite broad. I was mainly thinking about one question: **if AI can already generate working code, is it still necessary for me to learn programming?** As I developed the essay, I gradually connected this question to my own experience as a designer, especially my undergraduate graduation project. I also explored ideas from Dylan Beattie, Ada Lovelace, Alan Turing, and Don Ihde, and selected the ideas that were most relevant to my own thinking to support and develop my argument.

While discussing possible directions for the essay with AI, one suggestion I found useful was to think more carefully about the difference between **“working code” and “a successful interaction.”** This helped me connect the assignment question to my graduation project. Even if AI can generate code that makes a clap trigger sound and visual feedback, I still need to judge the timing, sensitivity, and relationship between different forms of feedback based on the experience I want to create. This idea eventually became an important part of my main argument.

One argument suggested by AI that I decided not to use was that **“human creativity cannot be replaced by AI.”** I felt this claim was too broad and did not accurately represent my position. I am comfortable using AI as part of my design process. What matters more to me is understanding enough about how programming works to question and modify what AI generates, and to make my own decisions about which results I ultimately choose to use.

During the final revision, I also used AI to help review the essay and cut around **350 words** to bring it closer to the required word limit. Rather than simply shortening sentences, I removed repeated explanations and kept the parts that most directly supported my main argument and personal experience.
